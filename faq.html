FAQ

Psilocybin questions and answers

Status: Services are pending licensure under the New Mexico Medical Psilocybin Program. No services are available at this time.

Last updated October 14, 2026. New Mexico is still finalizing its rules, so some answers may change.

Does Thirty-Sixth Parallel LLC provide psilocybin services now?

Not yet. Services are pending licensure under the New Mexico Medical Psilocybin Program. No services are available at this time, and consultation requests are not open yet.

What is the New Mexico Medical Psilocybin Program?

It is New Mexico's state program for the medical use of psilocybin. The program is medical, so every participant needs a qualifying diagnosis. The program is expected to begin in January 2027. The state is still finalizing its rules, so the details on this page may change.

Who qualifies for psilocybin treatment in New Mexico?

Under the proposed rules, every participant, in state or out of state, needs a qualifying diagnosis: treatment-resistant depression, a substance use disorder (including cannabis or nicotine use disorders), post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), or end-of-life care. Colorado's program does not have a diagnosis requirement.

Do I have to live in New Mexico?

No. Under the proposed rules, the qualifying-diagnosis requirement applies to every participant, in state or out of state, which means people from outside New Mexico may take part if they qualify. Check the final rules once the state publishes them.

How is the New Mexico process different from Colorado's?

New Mexico is expected to require a telehealth evaluation appointment to confirm a qualifying diagnosis before treatment begins, and may require a second staff member present during the medicine session. Preparation and integration sessions are part of the process in both states.

Is psilocybin legal in Colorado?

Yes, within Colorado's regulated program. Colorado voters passed Proposition 122 in 2022, creating a regulated program for the supervised therapeutic use of psilocybin. Sessions take place at state-licensed healing centers with state-licensed facilitators. It is supervised therapeutic use, not recreational legalization.

Is psilocybin right for everyone?

No. There are specific contraindications, including a personal or family history of psychosis or schizophrenia spectrum disorders, a family history of bipolar I disorder, certain medications (especially lithium), pregnancy, and heart conditions. A careful screening process comes before any psilocybin session.

Do I need prior psychedelic experience?

No. Many people who take part have little or no prior experience with psychedelics. Preparation sessions are designed to build the understanding and internal resources needed to approach the experience safely and with intention. Good preparation and a clinically trained provider matter more than prior experience.

Can psilocybin help with perimenopause or menopause?

Research in this area is early. Emerging research and clinical experience suggest psilocybin may help with the psychological and emotional dimensions of perimenopause and menopause, such as mood changes, anxiety, depression, disrupted identity, and grief. It is not a treatment for physical symptoms such as hot flashes. Under New Mexico's program, treatment requires one of the qualifying diagnoses listed above.

How much will psilocybin treatment cost?

Pricing has not been set. It will be set once the company is licensed and the state's rules are final. Because of the expected evaluation appointment and staffing requirements, New Mexico treatment may cost more than Colorado's program.
